What is a per-cabin charter?

Book a cabin, not the whole boat.

A per-cabin charter lets you reserve your own private cabin on a fully crewed sailing yacht in the British Virgin Islands — instead of chartering the entire vessel. You enjoy the comfort and freedom of life at sea, shared with a small group of like-minded travellers.

It's the most accessible way to experience a crewed luxury catamaran in the BVI — without the cost, complexity, or commitment of booking the whole boat.

A sample week

Seven days
you'll always remember.

No two charters are the same — your itinerary, menus, and activities adapt to weather, sea conditions, and the people on board. This is a sample of how a typical week unfolds.

01
Day 01 · Welcome aboard

Welcome aboard.

Step aboard at Tortola, meet your captain and chef, and feel the first breeze fill the sails. A short hop to Deadman's Bay on Peter Island — your first swim straight off the back of the boat, sunset drinks on deck, a chef-prepared welcome dinner under the stars.

  • First sail to a sheltered anchorage
  • Sunset drinks on deck
  • Welcome dinner under the stars
Fun factDeadman's Bay is named after pirate legends — today, the only thing washing up is you, drink in hand.
02
Day 02 · Snorkels & caves

Snorkels & caves.

World-class snorkelling at coral gardens like The Indians — vibrant reef fish, water you can see your hand in twenty feet down. Afternoon at Treasure Caves on Norman Island, said to have inspired Stevenson's Treasure Island. Evening at anchor in The Bight.

  • Snorkel iconic BVI reef sites
  • Lunch at a quiet cove
  • Evening at anchor in a hidden bight
Did you knowNorman Island inspired Treasure Island — and real treasure has been found in these waters.
03
Day 03 · Beach day & beach bars

Beach day, beach bars.

Soft sand, shallow turquoise, hammocks and barefoot bars on Jost Van Dyke. Anchor at Sandy Cay, swim ashore at White Bay — the kind of place that ruins other beaches for you. Optional evenings at legendary spots like the Soggy Dollar and Foxy's.

  • Anchor at uninhabited Sandy Cay
  • Painkillers at the Soggy Dollar Bar
  • Optional evening ashore at Foxy's
Fun factThe Soggy Dollar got its name from guests swimming ashore with cash in their pockets.
04
Day 04 · Wild islands & resort evening

Wild islands, resort evening.

Scenic passages along Tortola's green north shore — open water, dolphins, flying fish. Snorkel at protected reserves like Monkey Point on Guana Island, then a relaxed evening ashore at Scrub or Marina Cay. Pool, freshwater showers, dinner with feet on dry land.

  • Snorkel a protected nature reserve
  • Sail Tortola's scenic north shore
  • First evening ashore at a small resort
Fun factGuana Island is a protected nature reserve — its snorkelling is often compared to a natural aquarium.
05
Day 05 · The Baths & North Sound

The Baths & North Sound.

The Baths on Virgin Gorda are unlike anywhere else — granite boulders the size of houses forming caves, tunnels, and shallow pools that catch the light. Snorkel at Fallen Jerusalem, then sail through the BVI's most sheltered waters into North Sound, dotted with small resorts.

  • Walk through the boulders at The Baths
  • Snorkel at Fallen Jerusalem
  • Sunset drinks at Saba Rock
Fun factThe Baths' granite boulders were formed millions of years ago, sculpted by sea and weather.
06
Day 06 · The outer edge

The outer edge.

An open-water sail to Anegada, the BVI's only coral atoll. Flat, remote, and ringed by reef — wide empty beaches, pink flamingos in the salt ponds, and lobster grilled on the beach as the sun goes down. Stars at anchor like nowhere else in the chain.

  • Spot flamingos at the salt ponds
  • Wide empty beaches like Cow Wreck
  • Classic Anegada lobster dinner
Fun factAnegada means "drowned land" — its highest point is just 8–9 metres above sea level.
07
Day 07 · Last splashes

Last splashes.

A final long sail down the Sir Francis Drake Channel. Morning snorkel at one last reef, then onward to Cooper Island — an eco-focused beach club with a solar-powered brewery, reef-safe everything, and the most considered evening of the week.

  • Sail the Sir Francis Drake Channel
  • Final snorkel at a quiet point
  • Farewell dinner at anchor
Fun factCooper Island Beach Club runs a solar-powered microbrewery — one of the most sustainable in the Caribbean.
08
Day 08 · Homeward bound

Homeward bound.

Final breakfast on deck. A short, easy sail back to base. Time for last photos and proper goodbyes. You step ashore sun-kissed, slightly slower of speech, and full of stories. Fair winds and following seas — until next time.

  • Breakfast under sail
  • Disembark by late morning
  • Transfers arranged on request
Fun fact"Fair winds and following seas" is a sailor's farewell — wishing you an easy journey until next time.

Is this trip for you?

A balance of
privacy and connection.

Per-cabin sailing isn't for everyone — and that's the point. It's for travellers who value experience over schedule, and quiet bays over crowded resorts.

This is for you if

  • You're travelling as a couple, solo, or with friends
  • You're a family or group looking to share a yacht and split costs
  • You'd rather book one cabin than organise an entire boat
  • You value experience over a strict schedule
  • You're comfortable going with the flow

Probably not for you if

  • You want a rigid, hour-by-hour itinerary
  • You're after a nightlife-heavy, party-first holiday
  • You're looking for a cruise or all-inclusive resort experience
  • You'd prefer your own private vessel from the start
